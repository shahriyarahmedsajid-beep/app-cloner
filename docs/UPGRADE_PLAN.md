# Zupona AI — Upgrade Plan 2026

**Status:** Decision draft for the next product cycle  
**Date:** 21 October 2026  
**Owner:** Product / Design (concept team)  
**Related:** [Competitive research](./RESEARCH.md) · [PRD](./PRD.md) · [Live prototype](../README.md)

> Prices, model names and feature availability move weekly. This document is a point-in-time synthesis of public product pages and release notes. Treat every number as directional. Zupona is an independent concept and is not affiliated with any company named here.

---

## 1. The decision

Do not compete on model access. That race is over.

Higgsfield, InVideo, Runway, Krea, Freepik, Adobe and Google all now offer breadth, reference images, agents and 30-second generation. Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5 and Veo 3.1 have made capability parity a moving target that no aggregator can own for long.

**Zupona should compete on production accountability.**

> **Positioning:** Zupona is the campaign compiler for people who ship. Give it a brief; it returns a budgeted, continuity-locked production plan, routes every shot to the right model, and gives you a receipt for what it cost and where it came from.

This is narrower than "AI creative suite" and much harder to copy than another model picker. It turns the strongest themes in the research — outcome-first workflows, cost clarity, continuity and trust — into one product surface.

**The flagship experience: Zupona Director.**

```
Brief → Outcome contract → Production plan → Storyboard → Review → Deliverable
                              ↑                    ↓
                       Continuity ledger      Cost envelope
```

The user does not start with a model. They start with a result, a budget and a deadline. Zupona compiles the rest.

## 2. What changed since the original research

The first research brief covered Higgsfield, InVideo, Kling and Google Flow. The market has since moved in five important ways:

1. **Agents became table stakes.** Higgsfield has Supercomputer ("plans, generates, and assembles finished work"). Adobe has Firefly AI Assistant and Creative Skills. Runway has Agent inside Projects. Creatify has a Creative Agent. InVideo has Agent Two. "Chat to create" is no longer a differentiator.
2. **Reference control exploded.** Kling 4.0 supports up to 15 reference assets, 10 keyframes, 3–30 second takes, 4K and 8,000-token prompts. Runway's Seedance 2.5 page advertises up to 50 references and 30-second multi-shot video. Veo 3.1 supports ingredients, first/last frames and extension. Reference images are becoming a commodity.
3. **30 seconds is the new 5 seconds.** Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5 and Runway all push native 30-second output. Longer clips increase the value of sequence-level continuity, not clip-level novelty.
4. **Brand memory is forming inside silos.** Adobe is testing Elements (saved characters, locations and objects) and Projects (persistent context). Runway Agent sessions can access Brand Kits inside Projects. Higgsfield markets a single canvas for workflows. Each is a walled garden.
5. **Sora exited as a product.** OpenAI's public Sora pages state that, as of 26 April 2026, the Sora product is no longer available. Model availability is volatile; a model-agnostic workflow layer is more durable than any single provider bet.

The strategic implication is not "add agents." It is "own the layer above the agent: the production contract."

## 4. Table stakes vs. whitespace

### 4.1 Table stakes by the next buying cycle

- Multi-model access and a model picker.
- Text, image, video and audio references.
- 10–30 second output and 4K finishing.
- A conversational agent.
- Brand kits and saved characters/assets.
- Storyboards and light editing.
- C2PA or visible AI disclosure on some exports.
- MCP/API access for external agents.
- AI actor or avatar libraries for ads.

Copying these is necessary, not differentiating. They should be bought, integrated or matched, not used as the headline.

### 4.2 Whitespace Zupona can own

1. **The outcome contract.** A brief becomes explicit deliverables, budget, deadline, locks and acceptance criteria before generation.
2. **The continuity ledger.** Identity, product and world rules persist across shots, ratios and providers, with a visible drift estimate and repair path.
3. **The cost envelope.** One budget for the whole campaign, not a surprise bill per render. The compiler trades route quality against budget before the user commits.
4. **The route receipt.** Every shot explains why it used a model, what it cost and what alternatives existed.
5. **The review room that executes.** Timecoded feedback becomes scoped, priced instructions the agent can apply.
6. **The taste graph.** Accepted and rejected outputs with reasons become a durable, workspace-specific creative asset.
7. **Branching productions.** Explore a variant without endangering an approved campaign; diff, merge and roll back.
8. **The rights passport.** C2PA, consent, model terms and AI disclosure travel with the asset, not buried in a help centre.
9. **Marketplace lineage.** Remixable outcomes with acceptance rates and cost history, not prompt packs.
10. **The outcome guarantee.** If a render misses the contract, credits return automatically. Trust as a product feature.

## 5. The flagship: Zupona Director

### 5.1 Promise

> **One brief. A whole campaign. A receipt.**

Director is a campaign compiler, not a chat box. It is designed for the moment after "we need a launch campaign by Friday" and before anyone opens five tabs.

### 5.2 The five objects

| Object | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Brief** | The outcome, audience, budget, deadline and mandatory locks | Starts from the job, not the model |
| **Outcome contract** | Deliverables, cost cap, revision policy, acceptance criteria | Makes "done" explicit |
| **Production plan** | Ordered beats, each with a route, prompt, aspect, duration and cost | Turns intent into an inspectable plan |
| **Continuity ledger** | Product, cast, brand and world locks with a drift estimate | Makes consistency a first-class state |
| **Route receipt** | Per-shot model, rationale, cost, alternate and provenance | Makes the bill and the decisions legible |

### 5.3 The flow

1. **Brief.** User writes one paragraph, picks an objective, a budget and the things that must not drift.
2. **Compile.** Zupona returns a contract and a shot plan. No credits are spent.
3. **Inspect.** User sees route logic, cost per beat, budget headroom and continuity risk. They can swap a route or trim a beat.
4. **Storyboard.** Render low-cost board frames. The plan shows which shots are drafts and which are finals.
5. **Review.** Comments become instructions with a cost preview. Approve, reject with a reason or branch.
6. **Deliver.** Export the approved sequence with a cost receipt, rights passport and provenance metadata.

### 5.4 What makes it feel different

- **The receipt is the hero.** A campaign has a line-item bill and a route rationale, not a credit counter.
- **The compiler pushes back.** It warns when the budget cannot support the requested quality and offers a trade, rather than silently failing.
- **Locks are visible.** The user sees exactly which attributes are protected across every beat.
- **Every shot has a reason.** No black-box "best model" claim.
- **The board looks like production, not a dashboard.** Contact sheet, call sheets, stamps, route lines.

## 6. The ten upgrades, ranked

### P0 — build now / next

#### 1. Campaign Director
**What:** Brief-to-campaign compiler with objective presets, shot beats, route assignments, budget and continuity locks.  
**Why:** Creates the category and the demo moment. Nothing in the competitive set packages this as one outcome.  
**Acceptance:** A user can compile a five-beat campaign, see the full cost, render a board and download the plan without leaving the page.

#### 2. Continuity Ledger
**What:** Workspace-level locks for product truth, cast identity, brand system and world grammar; drift estimate per shot; repair suggestions.  
**Why:** Kling, Veo and Adobe are validating reference control, but inside silos without a portable contract.  
**Acceptance:** Every shot inherits active locks; removing a lock changes the drift estimate and flags the affected shots.

#### 3. Cost Envelope + Route Receipt
**What:** Campaign budget, line-item costs, headroom, downgrade/upgrade suggestions and a plain-language reason for every route.  
**Why:** Credit anxiety is the category's most consistent complaint. Runway has project caps; nobody makes the trade-off legible before generation.  
**Acceptance:** Cost is visible before any render; the compiler never silently exceeds the cap; each shot has a rationale.

#### 4. Model Duel
**What:** Run two routes on the same locked brief, side by side, with cost, latency, quality proxy and provider terms. Accept one; the other becomes a rejected data point.  
**Why:** Aggregators route invisibly. A visible, controlled comparison turns routing into a user benefit.  
**Acceptance:** Two drafts render from the same locks; the accepted take becomes the canonical shot; the rejected take is recorded with a reason.

#### 5. Review Room
**What:** Shareable review link with frame-accurate comments. A comment can become an agent instruction with a cost estimate before it runs.  
**Why:** Adobe and Runway have comments; neither closes the loop from feedback to priced execution.  
**Acceptance:** A reviewer can approve a shot or leave a scoped note; the owner can apply the note and see the cost delta.

### P1 — beta differentiators

#### 6. Taste Graph
**What:** A private map of accepted/rejected assets, reasons and brand rules. It updates route scoring and prompt compilation per workspace.  
**Why:** Adobe learns preferences; Arcads/Creatify optimize performance. Zupona can make approval reasons portable across models.  
**Acceptance:** After ten decisions, the next plan cites workspace preferences and avoids previously rejected patterns.

#### 7. Branching Productions
**What:** Git-like campaign branches: branch from an approved shot, change one variable, compare, merge.  
**Why:** Creative work is iterative but current tools overwrite context. Runway Projects is close, but not branch-native.  
**Acceptance:** A user can branch a campaign, change the hook, compare costs and merge the approved branch without losing the original.

#### 8. Rights Passport
**What:** Per-asset record of C2PA provenance, consent, provider terms, model version, allowed channels, expiry and AI-disclosure requirement.  
**Why:** C2PA is becoming table stakes at the model layer. Commercial teams need it at the campaign layer.  
**Acceptance:** Every export carries a human-readable rights summary and machine-readable provenance.

#### 9. Marketplace 2.0
**What:** Outcome recipes with lineage, cost history, acceptance rate and creator royalties. Buyers see the full production graph before running.  
**Why:** Existing marketplaces are prompt packs or app galleries. A recipe with a proven acceptance rate is a better unit.  
**Acceptance:** Every listing shows inputs, route alternatives, median cost, acceptance rate and remix terms.

#### 10. Outcome Guarantee
**What:** If an output fails the contract criteria (e.g. product label unreadable, wrong aspect, identity drift above threshold), credits are refunded automatically.  
**Why:** Trust is scarce. A guarantee is a stronger signal than another "premium quality" claim.  
**Acceptance:** A failed acceptance check creates a ledger refund with a reason, without a support ticket.

### P2 — expansion

- **Live co-direction canvas:** realtime sketching while locks, routes and costs stay visible. Krea owns exploration; Zupona can own *production-grade* realtime.
- **Performance loop:** connect ad accounts, learn which hooks and routes convert, and propose the next batch.
- **Agent API / MCP:** let external agents compile and run Zupona campaigns through the same contract.
- **Team planning:** capacity, calendars, clients and budget pools.
- **Localisation at the campaign level:** one contract, many markets, one cost envelope.

## 7. What not to build

- **Another model catalogue as the homepage.** It is already a commodity.
- **Another generic chat agent.** Higgsfield, Adobe, Runway and InVideo have one.
- **A full non-linear editor.** Integrate and hand off; do not rebuild Premiere.
- **An open prompt marketplace.** Prompt packs are low-signal. Curated outcome recipes are the unit.
- **Unlimited video.** It destroys margin and attracts abuse.
- **Fake social proof or benchmark claims.** The category is already noisy; trust is the brand.
- **A social feed.** Discovery belongs in the storefront and review room, not a content network.
- **Celebrity likeness tools.** High legal risk, low strategic fit.

## 8. Product and design direction

### 8.1 New information architecture

```
/                     Marketing homepage
/director             Campaign compiler (new flagship)
/store                Outcome storefront
/store/[slug]         Workflow detail
/studio               Manual canvas
/library              Assets and campaigns
/brand                Brand kit and continuity locks
/pricing              Plans and cost examples
/research             Research + upgrade plan summary
```

**Primary navigation:** Director · Storefront · Studio · Library · Pricing.

The homepage should lead with the campaign promise, not a model roster. The model roster stays as proof, not the headline.

### 8.2 The Director visual language

The current site is a strong dark editorial base, but it risks becoming another lime-on-black AI startup. Director should introduce a distinct production grammar:

- **Contact sheet, not card grid.** Shots arranged like a storyboard with beat numbers, route lines and approval stamps.
- **Paper contract.** A bone-coloured contract/receipt panel with tabular numerals and a perforated edge. This is the one intentional light surface.
- **Route lines.** Thin lime rules connect brief → contract → shot → receipt.
- **Fewer glass panels.** More rules, labels and whitespace. The interface should feel like a director's folder, not a dashboard.
- **Contrast floor.** Body copy moves from `white/30–40` to `white/60–70`; metadata stays secondary but readable.
- **No generic gradient hero.** Use a typographic statement and a real product artefact.
- **Motion with meaning.** Progress, route selection and approval are the only animated moments.

### 8.3 Homepage narrative

1. **Hero:** "One brief. A whole campaign. A receipt." → Try Director / Browse outcomes.
2. **Live artefact:** a real Director plan with five beats, locks, routes and a budget.
3. **Why now:** models are commodities; production accountability is not.
4. **Storefront:** outcome workflows.
5. **Studio:** manual control for the moments that need it.
6. **Trust:** cost, rights, continuity.
7. **Pricing:** campaigns, not credit roulette.

## 9. Implementation plan

### Phase 0 — Demo (this cycle)

Ship a working `/director` experience on top of the existing simulated API.

- Brief presets and free-text brief.
- Objective, budget, aspect and continuity-lock controls.
- Deterministic campaign compiler with 4–5 beats and cost-aware route selection.
- Campaign contract, route receipt, cost envelope and continuity confidence.
- Storyboard rendering through the existing `/api/generate` demo pipeline.
- Approve/reject states, plan download, library save and reset path.
- Navigation, homepage entry, SEO metadata and sitemap entry.
- Demo honesty: every simulated frame is labelled; no real model call.

**Success criteria for the demo:** a first-time visitor understands the promise in under 10 seconds, compiles a campaign in under 60 seconds, sees the full cost before rendering, and can explain what makes it different from a model aggregator.

### Phase 1 — Production foundation (weeks 4–10)

- Persist briefs, contracts, plans, shots, locks and approvals in Postgres.
- Replace the deterministic compiler with a planning model plus deterministic cost guard.
- Add server-side credit ledger, reservation and automatic refund.
- Implement two real adapters: one fast video route and one premium video route, plus two image routes.
- Add reference uploads, consent records and a first continuity embedding.
- Generate C2PA provenance on export.
- Wire analytics events for compile, route change, render, approval and download.

### Phase 2 — Private alpha (weeks 10–16)

- Review links and comments.
- Model Duel.
- Taste Graph v1 from approval reasons.
- Branching v1 for shots.
- 3 flagship outcomes: launch film, UGC ad set, product drop.
- Instrument cost per accepted shot and acceptance rate by route.

### Phase 3 — Public beta (weeks 16–24)

- Campaign history and team workspaces.
- Rights Passport v1.
- Public rate card and cost simulator.
- Marketplace pilot with 12 curated creators.
- Pricing calibrated against p50 inference costs with a margin floor.

### Phase 4 — Expansion (months 6–12)

- Outcome Guarantee.
- Performance loop.
- Agent API / MCP.
- Live co-direction canvas.
- Enterprise controls and regional routing.

## 10. Technical shape

### 10.1 Core types

```ts
type CampaignBrief = {
  brief: string;
  objective: "launch" | "ugc" | "drop" | "series";
  budgetCredits: number;
  aspect: "1:1" | "4:5" | "16:9" | "9:16";
  locks: ("product" | "character" | "brand" | "world")[];
  deadline?: string;
};

type Shot = {
  id: string;
  beat: string;
  direction: string;
  prompt: string;
  modelId: string;
  mode: "image" | "video";
  duration: 5 | 10;
  credits: number;
  rationale: string;
  risk: "low" | "medium" | "high";
};

type ProductionPlan = {
  id: string;
  contract: string[];
  shots: Shot[];
  totalCredits: number;
  headroom: number;
  continuityConfidence: number;
  routeReceipt: RouteReceipt[];
};
```

### 10.2 Compiler rules

1. **Validate the brief.** Minimum useful words, objective, budget and aspect.
2. **Build beats from the objective.** Launch, UGC, drop and series have different grammars.
3. **Choose a default route per beat.** Fast route for hooks, image route for product truth, premium route for the close.
4. **Apply locks.** Append continuity instructions to every prompt and mark protected attributes.
5. **Enforce the budget.** Downgrade premium routes before dropping a beat; never silently exceed the cap.
6. **Explain the plan.** Every shot gets a route rationale and an alternate.
7. **Estimate risk.** Show a confidence number, clearly labelled as a demo estimate, and list the shots most likely to drift.
8. **Freeze the plan.** A compiled plan is immutable; edits create a new version.

In production, steps 2–3 can use a planner model, but steps 1, 4, 5 and 8 stay deterministic and auditable. The cost guard must never be delegated to an LLM.

### 10.3 Execution

- Each shot is an independent generation job with the plan ID and shot ID attached.
- Credits are reserved at compile/run time, captured per successful shot and released on failure.
- The board can render drafts first (cheap routes) and finals later (premium routes).
- Approved shots are immutable; a change creates a branch.
- Every job stores provider, model version, request hash, cost, latency and provenance.
- The receipt is generated from the ledger, not from client state.

## 11. Metrics

### North-star candidate

**Approved campaign assets per active workspace per month.** It captures whether Zupona is producing work people actually ship, not just generations.

### Funnel

| Stage | Metric | Target (beta) |
|---|---|---|
| Understand | Visitor can state the Director promise unaided | ≥ 70% in usability tests |
| Compile | New visitors who compile a campaign | ≥ 40% |
| Render | Compiled campaigns that render a board | ≥ 55% |
| Accept | Shots approved without a re-render | ≥ 35% |
| Repeat | Workspaces compiling a second campaign in 14 days | ≥ 25% |
| Trust | Cost variance vs. quoted envelope | ≤ 2% |
| Speed | Brief → approved board | < 10 minutes |
| Economics | Contribution margin after inference | ≥ 55% |

### Guardrails

- Cost per accepted asset, not cost per render.
- Refund and billing-complaint rate.
- Route failure and fallback rate.
- Time spent in review per approved shot.
- Percentage of shots with a continuity warning.
- Model concentration risk.
- Support tickets per 1,000 shots.

## 12. Experiments

1. **Hero copy:** "One brief. A whole campaign. A receipt." vs. "Your imagination, with a ship date."
2. **Default budget:** 60 vs. 120 credits. Does a tighter envelope increase or reduce render completion?
3. **Contract first vs. plan first.** Does showing the contract before the shot list increase trust?
4. **Route receipt detail.** Full rationale vs. one-line reason. Measure compile-to-render.
5. **Continuity confidence.** Percentage vs. risk bands. Avoid false precision.
6. **Draft-first vs. final-first.** Does a cheap board increase approved output per credit?
7. **Review link placement.** At render completion vs. after approval.
8. **Pricing:** campaign bundles vs. credit packs.

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compiler produces generic plans | High | High | Curate objectives; use approval data; human QA; narrow launch outcomes |
| Users do not trust a confidence score | Medium | Medium | Label as estimate; show drivers; avoid false precision; benchmark against accepted outputs |
| Budget-aware routing feels cheap | Medium | High | Offer explicit upgrades; show quality trade-off; lock premium beats |
| Continuity promise exceeds reality | High | High | Narrow supported cases; reference requirements; drift warnings; refund guarantee later |
| Review loop creates scope creep | Medium | Medium | Scoped instructions, cost preview, approval gate |
| Model providers bundle the same workflow | High | High | Own brand memory, taste graph, marketplace and cross-model receipt |
| Rights passport becomes legal overclaim | Medium | High | Plain-language summary; provider terms linked; legal review |
| Cost guard bugs create billing disputes | Medium | High | Server-authoritative ledger; reservation; audit log; no auto-overage |
| Too many surfaces dilute the story | High | Medium | Director is the front door; storefront and studio support it |

## 15. Open decisions

1. Is **Director** the right product name, or should it be **Campaign Compiler** / **Production Room**?
2. Which launch objective gets the first production-grade workflow: launch film, UGC ad set, product drop or character series?
3. Does the free tier get one compiled campaign per month, or compile-only with paid rendering?
4. Should the contract expose a deadline and revision count in v1, or only budget and locks?
5. How much of continuity can be honest without fine-tuning at launch?
6. Is the right first proof point a cost guarantee, an acceptance guarantee or a rights passport?
7. Should the initial marketplace be curated outcomes only, or allow creator recipes with a quality gate?
8. Which metrics justify killing the Director thesis? Proposed: if fewer than 25% of compiled campaigns render within 24 hours, revisit the brief-to-plan experience before adding features.
